Hospira BAC water, explained before you buy

Hospira BAC water is Bacteriostatic Water for Injection, USP: sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol added as a preservative, supplied in multiple-dose vials. The benzyl alcohol is what separates it from plain sterile water, which has no preservative and is meant for a single use.

Before you order

Vial and order checklist

Any listing for a prescription-labeled product deserves a check. Use this list on every vial you receive.

  • Know who you are buying from. Look for a legal business name, address and working support contact before you pay.
  • Read the vial label. It should show the product name, 0.9% benzyl alcohol, volume, lot number, expiration date and an NDC number.
  • Match the NDC. Look the number up in the FDA's NDC directory or DailyMed and confirm the product and manufacturer match.
  • Inspect the packaging. The cap should be intact and the liquid clear with no particles. Do not use a vial that looks wrong.
  • Check expiry and storage. Note the date and the storage line on the label.
  • Keep the order record. Save the receipt, lot number and photos in case of a recall or a problem.

Questions

Hospira bac water FAQ

What is Hospira bac water?

Hospira bac water is shorthand for Hospira Bacteriostatic Water for Injection, USP. It is sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol as a preservative, in a multiple-dose vial.

What is the difference between bacteriostatic water and sterile water?

Bacteriostatic water contains benzyl alcohol and is labeled for multiple-dose vials. Sterile water for injection has no preservative and is a single-use product.

Does CVS sell bacteriostatic water?

Is bacteriostatic water safe for everyone?

No. The label warns against use in newborns because of the benzyl alcohol. Ask a clinician or pharmacist about your situation.

How long does an opened vial last?

Follow the discard-after-opening statement printed on your vial's label and the guidance of a licensed professional. This page does not set a time limit.

What does 0.9% benzyl alcohol mean?

For the 30 mL Hospira vial, 0.9% means 9 mg of benzyl alcohol in each mL of water. It is the preservative that makes multiple-dose use possible.

Does bacteriostatic water contain sodium chloride?

No. Bacteriostatic Water for Injection is water with benzyl alcohol. Bacteriostatic Sodium Chloride Injection is a separate product with saline.

Is bacteriostatic water a medication?

It is classified as a prescription drug product, but its labeled function is as a diluent or solvent for drugs that need to be diluted or dissolved. It is not meant to be used on its own.

What does "USP" and "nonpyrogenic" mean on the label?

USP means United States Pharmacopeia, the standard the product is made to. Nonpyrogenic means it is manufactured to meet limits on pyrogens, substances that can cause a fever response.

Does the expiration date vary?

Yes. Expiration dates and lot numbers differ by lot. Check the date printed on the vial and carton you receive.

Glossary

Terms used on this page

Inhibits bacterial growth without necessarily killing bacteria.

The preservative in bacteriostatic water, present at 0.9%.
Diluent
A liquid used to dilute or dissolve another substance.
Multiple-dose vial
A vial designed to be entered more than once, which is why it contains a preservative.

USP
